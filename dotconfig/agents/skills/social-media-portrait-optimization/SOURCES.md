# Source ledger: creator teachings and evidence

Research accessed **2026-09-23**. Publication and modification dates below describe the source, not when this skill was written. Advice is paraphrased except for short quoted anchors. Locators identify headings or searchable phrases; web layouts can change.

## How to use this ledger

- **Direct teaching:** what a creator or practitioner wrote or said. Useful experience, not automatically causal evidence.
- **Reported teaching:** an interview or quotation hosted by another author; keep that provenance in the attribution.
- **Empirical / research report:** findings about a defined task and sample, with limits on generalization.
- **Editorial example / testimonial:** a description or endorsement, not the pictured person's own technical teaching.
- **Official specification:** a dated platform requirement; refresh for current exports.

Creator advice, observational findings, and the skill's own audit synthesis answer different questions. Agreement across practitioners can motivate a sensible default without establishing a universal law.

## Dan Koe

### DK01 — Intentionally design the feeling and experience

- **Source:** [How To Create A Life Of Meaning, Money, & Impact (As Fast As Humanly Possible)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/how-to-create-a-life-of-meaning-money-impact-as-fast-as-humanly-possible/)
- **Published:** 2022-05-28. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** concluding application to branding; vision board, black-and-white skyscrapers, and “It's about the experience.”
- **Teaching:** gather visual references that reflect your own taste and the feeling/environment you want to create. Koe relates his black-and-white skyscraper imagery to his taste and earlier aspirations of living in a city; he asks readers to consider profile pictures and colors as part of the desired experience.
- **Use:** establish aesthetic intent before prescribing a palette or photographic style.
- **Limit:** an explanation of personal taste is not a universal recommendation for monochrome or architecture backgrounds.

### DK02 — Make a followable profile, then move on

- **Source:** [The Simple 3-Point Online Business Model](https://thedankoe.com/letters/the-simple-3-point-online-business-model/)
- **Published:** 2022-03-01. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** “Craft A Followable Profile.”
- **Teaching:** use a good-looking profile photo, a compelling reader-oriented bio, and a link to the offer. Ask whether the profile looks like it could have 100,000 followers; he explicitly cautions against overthinking it.
- **Use:** present the account as intentional and worth following.
- **Limit:** the follower-count comparison is a positioning heuristic, not a forecast, a requirement to look famous, or a validated portrait checklist.

### DK03 — Make the profile picture clickable

- **Sources:** [The Rise Of The Value Creator (A New Career Path)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/the-rise-of-the-value-creator-a-new-career-path/) (2022-10-15); [The Value Creator (A New Internet Career Path For Intelligent People)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/the-value-creator-a-new-internet-career-path-for-intelligent-people/) (2023-07-08).
- **Type:** direct teaching, repeated across related letters.
- **Locator:** “Have a clickable profile picture so people know you are a creator.”
- **Teaching:** use the image to signal a deliberate creator presence and invite further attention.
- **Limit:** neither passage provides an exact crop, expression, background, or color recipe. “Clear, recognizable, intriguing in context” is this skill's operational interpretation, not a Koe quotation. These two letters are not independent experiments.

### DK04 — Familiarity through repeated encounters

- **Source:** [How To Network With Millionaires And Build A Name For Yourself](https://thedankoe.com/letters/how-to-network-with-millionaires-and-build-a-name-for-yourself/)
- **Published:** 2023-01-28. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** following/engaging before DMs, around tips 7–8; familiarity with a face/profile picture.
- **Teaching:** engage with someone for a few days before approaching them so they have had opportunities to recognize you.
- **Use:** support the repeated-identity-cue model and the value of continuity.
- **Limit:** advice about networking familiarity, not a measured effect of changing or keeping an avatar.

### DK05 — A headshot helps; showing a face is optional

- **Source:** [Why Micro Education Businesses Are The Future (Zero Dollars To Start)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/why-micro-education-businesses-are-the-future-zero-dollars-to-start/)
- **Published:** 2023-09-02. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** writing platforms, showing face/body, and a headshot profile picture.
- **Teaching:** writing-led platforms do not require showing one's face or body, although a headshot profile picture helps.
- **Use:** a face is a practical default for a person-led brand, not a prerequisite that overrides pseudonymity or privacy preferences.

### DK06 — Learn enough, set up quickly, and test

- **Source:** [The Minimalist Creator Business (Make Your First $1,000 In 60 Days)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/the-minimalist-creator-business-make-your-first-1000-in-60-days/)
- **Published:** 2024-09-07. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** “Creating Your Profile.”
- **Teaching:** acquire basic photography/design knowledge, watch one to three headshot/profile-picture tutorials, then create and learn through execution instead of prolonged tutorial consumption.
- **Use:** prefer a concrete crop or short shot brief over a sprawling rebrand for someone getting started.
- **Limit:** the article's business-income headline is not evidence of portrait-driven earnings.

### DK07 — Brand is an environment accumulated over time

- **Source:** [The Death Of The Personal Brand (And What Comes Next)](https://thedankoe.com/letters/the-death-of-the-personal-brand-and-what-comes-next/)
- **Published:** 2024-09-28. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** “Brand Is An Environment.”
- **Teaching:** worldview, story, and philosophy should be felt across the picture, banner, bio, content, and landing pages. A brand develops in the audience's mind over months of contact.
- **Use:** assess coherence with the broader account when context is available.
- **Limit:** matching colors alone does not establish a coherent brand or audience trust.

### DK08 — Proportion: quality ideas matter more than profile polish

- **Source:** [How To Build A Better Personal Brand Than 99% Of People](https://thedankoe.com/letters/how-to-build-a-better-personal-brand-than-99-of-people/)
- **Published:** 2025-05-29. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** profile details, successful accounts with blank pictures/no bio, and consistent quality of ideas.
- **Teaching:** profile details have a role, but repeated quality ideas build trust; even sparse profiles can succeed.
- **Use:** stop optimizing once the portrait is fit for purpose and substantive work has higher value.
- **Limit:** this qualifies his profile advice; it does not establish that portraits never matter.

## Justin Welsh

### JW01 — Choose the first impression intentionally

- **Source:** [Most Social Profiles Aren't Very Good. Here's How To Fix Yours](https://www.justinwelsh.me/essays/most-social-profiles-arent-very-good-heres-how-to-fix-yours)
- **Published:** 2022-05-14. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Locator:** “Your headshot is the first trust builder.”
- **Teaching:** a headshot may suggest kindness, toughness, playfulness, power, empathy, or professionalism. Choose the intended feeling and align it with the brand. Welsh describes hiring a particular photographer to communicate confidence.
- **Use:** make the desired impression an explicit input to the audit.
- **Limit:** he says there is no objectively correct profile design. “Trust builder” describes communication, not a test of the subject's actual trustworthiness.

### JW02 — Clear, current, and emotionally appropriate

- **Source:** [Optimize Your LinkedIn For 2024](https://www.justinwelsh.me/essays/optimize-your-linkedin-for-2024)
- **Published:** 2024-01-27. **Type:** direct teaching with an editorial example.
- **Locator:** “Your Profile Photo.”
- **Teaching:** avoid blurry or outdated images; a professional photographer, a friend's iPhone portrait mode, and AI tools are possible routes. Choose the emotion the image should convey. He praises Jay Clouse's image for being warm and inviting for a creator-first business.
- **Use:** evaluate the delivered image and fit rather than assuming an expensive production method is necessary.
- **Limit:** the Clouse example is Welsh's interpretation, not advice independently authored by Clouse. Mentioning AI tools does not prove equivalent likeness or superior performance.

### JW03 — Invest in branding at the right stage

- **Source:** [Justin Welsh Rebrand](https://www.justinwelsh.me/essays/justin-welsh-rebrand)
- **Published:** 2023-12-02. **Type:** direct teaching, broader-brand context.
- **Teaching:** branding work can distract from content and products; investment becomes more appropriate after traction. Define the desired look and feel before paying for the work.
- **Use:** scale the audit's recommendations to the account's stage.
- **Limit:** a rebrand account, not a portrait experiment or universal spending threshold.

## Detailed practitioner advice

### AC01 — Andy Crestodina: nine practical profile-picture tips

- **Source:** [How to Take the Perfect Profile Picture: 9 Tips, Plus Some Research](https://www.orbitmedia.com/blog/perfect-profile-pictures-9-tips-plus-some-research/)
- **Published:** 2018-07-03; modified 2026-06-30. **Type:** direct practitioner teaching plus quoted guests and secondary research.
- **Locator:** nine numbered tips.
- **Teaching:** show your face; frame it neither too close nor too far; choose a smile suited to the profession; use contrasting colors; keep the background simple; test candidates; incorporate subtle branding; use the same headshot across professional profiles; consider a professional photographer.
- **Small-size guidance:** discusses feed images such as 50 × 50 pixels. The central point is thumbnail readability, not a permanent cross-platform size specification.
- **Continuity:** recommends keeping an image for a year or two so recognition accumulates. Treat this as a heuristic rather than mandatory replacement timing.
- **Guest provenance:** quotes Mark Schaefer on choosing voice/personality/strategy through the picture and on the difficulty of connecting with logos. Quotes photographer Zack Smith on comfortable environments and remaining true to the brand. Attribute these as comments quoted by Crestodina, not independently reviewed articles.
- **Limits:** the orange-versus-blue-platform rationale reflects interface context that should not be assumed for modern X. Professional-photography advice is stronger than Welsh's range of acceptable options. Research citations to Photofeeler are the same evidence family as PF01.

### GK01 — Guy Kawasaki: a clear, face-focused Twitter avatar

- **Source:** [Looking for Mr. Goodtweet: How to Pick Up Followers on Twitter](https://guykawasaki.com/looking-for-m-1/)
- **Published:** 2008-11-10. **Type:** direct teaching; historical platform context.
- **Locator:** “Tip 3: Create an effective avatar.”
- **Teaching:** use a simple, informal, face-focused photo; ensure clear exposure, correct red-eye, and crop for a postage-stamp display. Start with sufficient source resolution, stand out in the stream, and allow creative treatments.
- **Use:** enduring clarity and reduction principles.
- **Limit:** the 500 × 500 suggestion is historical, not current X guidance. Other old follower-acquisition tactics in this article are outside this skill's scope.

### GK02 — Kawasaki on focus, composition, and light

- **Source:** [The Science to Having an Attractive Profile Photo on LinkedIn](https://socialnomics.net/2014/12/08/the-science-to-having-an-attractive-profile-photo-on-linkedin/)
- **Published:** 2014-12-08. **Type:** Erik Qualman's report/interview with Kawasaki.
- **Teaching:** focus on the face, consider asymmetric/rule-of-thirds composition, face the light, and use a source of at least 600 pixels. The avatar supports identity and perceived likability, competence, and trust.
- **Use:** practical shooting/composition hypotheses.
- **Limit:** “science” in the headline does not make this a controlled experiment. Current circular crops may favor a different composition; 600 pixels is historical source-quality advice.

### PEF01 — Peg Fitzpatrick: memorable, coherent, maintained

- **Source:** [Tips for a Fabulous Social Media Profile](https://pegfitzpatrick.com/tips-for-a-fabulous-social-media-profile/)
- **Published:** 2012-05-07; modified 2014-10-15. **Type:** direct teaching.
- **Teaching:** use a clear, correctly sized avatar; create a unique and memorable profile with coherent branding; inventory profile URLs so a picture change propagates across accounts; review profiles periodically.
- **Use:** consistent deployment and maintenance.
- **Limit:** Google+ and other historical service details are not current platform instructions. Periodic review does not require changing a recognizable image.

### FH01 — Dickie Bush and Nicolas Cole: commercial testimonials

- **Source:** [Founder Headshots](https://founderheadshots.com/)
- **Date:** undated page, accessed 2026-09-23. **Type:** client testimonials hosted by a photography business.
- **Teaching available:** Bush calls a headshot a professional “thumbnail” and praises Paola's attention to detail. Cole describes a high-grade headshot as valuable to digital presence and an industry-leader impression. Katie Hardy praises a contemporary natural feel rather than generic corporate posing.

## Research and testing evidence

### PF01 — Photofeeler: perceived professional traits

- **Source:** [New Research Study Breaks Down “The Perfect Profile Photo”](https://blog.photofeeler.com/perfect-photo/)
- **Last updated:** 2023-01-09. **Type:** platform-published empirical analysis.
- **Reported sample:** over 60,000 ratings of 800 profile photos, evaluating perceived competence, likability, and influence.
- **Reported positive associations:** visible/unobstructed eyes, a teeth-showing smile, a mild squinch, defined jaw lighting, head/shoulder or torso framing, and moderate editing. Formal dress improved professional ratings.
- **Reported null/negative findings:** no significant effect for background setting or B&W in this analysis; extreme darkness and saturation performed poorly.
- **Use:** candidate-generation hypotheses for professional impressions, especially when warmth/competence/influence are relevant audience goals.
- **Limits:** ratings describe perception, not actual competence or personality. This analysis does not establish causal follower growth, universal audience preference, or an optimal wardrobe for every creator. Photo features may covary. A nonsignificant result is not proof that a feature never matters. Audience, setting, and task can differ from X.

### CS01 — Cyrus Shepard: a historical author-photo experiment

- **Source:** [How Optimizing My Ugly Google+ Pic Increased Free Traffic 35%](https://moz.com/blog/google-author-photos)
- **Published:** 2012-08-15. **Type:** original practitioner case study.
- **Teaching/result:** tested author photos with different crops/backgrounds in Google authorship search snippets and reported roughly a 35% CTR lift. Emphasizes context and the absence of a magic color.
- **Limits:** the author calls the testing non-scientific; ranking changes affected results. It concerns a historical search presentation, not an X feed or a randomized follower-conversion experiment. Use as motivation to test, not a promised effect size.

### APS01 — Different photos of the same face yield different impressions

- **Source:** [Same Face, Many First Impressions](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/same-face-many-first-impressions.html)
- **Published:** 2014-06-02. **Type:** Association for Psychological Science report on Alexander Todorov and Jenny Porter's research.
- **Underlying paper:** DOI [10.1177/0956797614532474](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614532474).
- **Finding reported:** different photographs of the same person can yield substantially different trait impressions; preferred photographs depend on context; still-photo judgments can mislead.
- **Use:** compare multiple frames and fit the choice to the task rather than treating any one shot as the person's objective appearance or character.
- **Limit:** this ledger reviewed APS reporting, not the full paper. Do not invent sample details or claim a social-growth experiment.

### BUF01 — Kevan Lee / Buffer: useful review, overlapping evidence

- **Source:** [The Best Profile Picture: Science, Research, and Psychology](https://buffer.com/resources/best-profile-picture-science-research-psychology/)
- **Published:** 2022-10-10. **Type:** secondary review.
- **Use:** an attribution trail to Photofeeler, Kawasaki, and other studies.
- **Limit:** repeats evidence already represented above and mixes dating/avatar findings with professional-profile advice. Read the original task before transferring a finding. This article is not another independent replication of PF01.

## Reconcile the useful disagreements

| Question | Evidence tension | Working decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much does the portrait matter? | Welsh emphasizes first impression; Koe emphasizes accumulated ideas and execution. | Remove meaningful presentation friction, then return to substantive work. |
| Must the person smile? | PF01 favors smiles on several professional-impression ratings; Welsh allows varied emotional positioning; editorial examples include serious creators. | Choose by intended impression; test warmth when it matters. |
| Must the background be plain or colorful? | Crestodina favors simplicity/contrast; PF01 found no significant setting effect. | Judge actual small-format readability and audience fit. |
| Is B&W worse? | Common color prescriptions coexist with PF01's nonsignificant B&W result and distinctive monochrome examples. | Preserve coherent monochrome when it reads well; compare when uncertain. |
| Must the image be professionally shot? | Crestodina recommends a professional; Welsh allows phone portraits and Koe stresses quick execution. | Solve quality problems with stage-appropriate resources. |
| Must it be a face? | Many practitioners favor faces; Koe explicitly allows writing-led businesses without showing one. | Default to a face for a person-led account while respecting the identity strategy. |
| Should it change regularly? | Profile maintenance matters; recognition benefits from continuity. | Review when useful; replace for a reason rather than a schedule. |

## Maintaining the knowledge base

For a new teaching, record the original URL, speaker/author, publication date when available, locator, actual claim, source class, and relevant limits. Follow derivative claims to originals when possible. Keep contradictory advice with its context rather than silently replacing it. Change the operational workflow only when the new material supports a better decision process.
