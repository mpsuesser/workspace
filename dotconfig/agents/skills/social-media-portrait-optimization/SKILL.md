# Social-media portrait optimization

Treat a portrait as a **repeated identity cue**: it must read at feed size, create an appropriate first impression, and become recognizable through repeated encounters. Optimize for the user's audience and purpose, rather than a universal attractive-photo formula.

This skill combines creator teachings with research on perceived impressions. Its audit workflow is an original synthesis, not a method endorsed by the cited creators. The evidence supports judgments about presentation and recognition; it does not establish a predictable follower-growth lift.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the decision

Identify the platform, audience, intended impression, and the actual choice: keep, recrop, retouch, reshoot, or choose among candidates. Use context already supplied. Ask only for missing information that could change the recommendation; otherwise state a reasonable assumption and proceed.

For an established account, consider how long the current avatar has been used and what recognizable elements it already owns. For a new account, prioritize a credible, legible starting image over expensive polish.

**Complete when:** the decision and audience are explicit, and consequential unknowns are identified.

### 2. Inspect the evidence available

For an audit, open the actual image or supplied profile screenshot. If the image is inaccessible, request an upload or accessible URL and offer only a provisional brief until it can be inspected. Label candidates consistently.

Inspect a large view and a circular thumbnail at plausible feed sizes. Use actual platform screenshots when available. Suggested diagnostic sizes are in AUDIT.md; they are test fixtures, not platform specifications. Check light and dark surroundings when relevant. If rendering tools are unavailable, distinguish your visual estimate from a preview you actually inspected.

Separate what is visible from what requires the original file, account context, or audience feedback. A screenshot can establish crop and contrast, but may conceal original resolution and editing artifacts.

For a research-only request, use SOURCES.md instead of requiring an image. Match every named attribution to its source and preserve the distinction between the creator's own teaching and another writer's description of that creator.

**Complete when:** every candidate has been inspected at the available scales, or the access limitation is explicit; research-only answers have attributable evidence.

### 3. Evaluate the portrait

Use AUDIT.md to assess:

1. **Legibility:** identifiable face, useful crop, readable eyes/expression, separation from surroundings.
2. **Audience fit:** the intended impression versus the impression the visible presentation may encourage.
3. **Distinctiveness:** one or two identity cues that survive a thumbnail.
4. **Continuity:** recognition across time, platforms, and the wider profile.
5. **Authenticity and technical quality:** current likeness, restrained editing, usable source and export.

Resolve tradeoffs in context. Smiling, direct gaze, saturated color, monochrome, formal clothes, and professional photography are options with different effects. None is a universal prerequisite. Preserve effective distinctive choices rather than homogenizing every portrait into a corporate headshot.

Describe **perceived presentation**, not the person's actual trustworthiness, competence, personality, or protected characteristics. Evaluate controllable photographic choices rather than rating attractiveness or recommending changes to anatomy.

**Complete when:** every dimension has a finding or an explicit uncertainty, and the most important tradeoff is resolved or assigned a test.

### 4. Make the decision actionable

Lead with a verdict: **keep**, **small edit**, **reshoot**, or **choose candidate X**. State what already works. Recommend the smallest change likely to solve the observed problem, with at most three immediate priorities.

For each priority use:

> **Observation → likely presentation effect → concrete action → confidence**

Attach an original source when invoking a creator or study. Label your own crop/edit judgments as analysis. High confidence in a visible crop problem does not imply high confidence in a growth outcome.

When comparing candidates, choose a winner for the stated goal. Explain when another candidate would win for a different audience. If meaningful uncertainty remains, specify the smallest useful comparison test using TESTING-AND-PLATFORMS.md.

**Complete when:** the user has a decision, a prioritized executable edit/shot brief, and a test only where it could resolve a real uncertainty.

### 5. Stop at useful quality

Koe and Welsh both caution against branding work displacing substantive work. A recognizable, legible, audience-appropriate portrait can be good enough. If remaining preferences are marginal, recommend keeping it and returning to publishing, conversations, or the offer.

For deliverable images, inspect the final circular preview, preserve the original, and check current destination requirements before export. State which checks were actually performed.

**Complete when:** the proposed work is proportionate to the account's stage and the final answer distinguishes observed defects, sourced principles, and untested predictions.

## Default audit output

Adapt the length to the request; avoid a full worksheet for a simple choice.

1. **Verdict:** one sentence with the principal reason.
2. **Keep:** the strongest existing identity/presentation cues.
3. **Priorities:** up to three observation–action pairs, each with confidence.
4. **Thumbnail/context check:** what was inspected and what remains uncertain.
5. **Next move:** a precise crop/edit/shot brief, candidate choice, or targeted test.

For a comprehensive audit, add a compact matrix covering all five dimensions. Use descriptive statuses such as strong / needs work / uncertain; a numeric score is not a validated predictor of follows or revenue.

## Attribution discipline

- Give the person's actual advice, its context, and a source link. Keep paraphrases distinct from quotations.
- A famous creator's current portrait is an example, not evidence that the creator recommends its lighting, gaze, or color treatment.
- Several articles repeating the same Photofeeler analysis are one evidence family.
- Dates and platform specifications in this knowledge base are snapshots. Recheck live sources for present-tense technical claims or a request for the latest advice.
